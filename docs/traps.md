# Courses and traps

A course is a JSON document in `courses/` (a link to [mg-deathrun-maps](../../mg-deathrun-maps), generated by its `tools/build_courses.py`; `courses/README.md` is the format). `dr_course_doc.gd` validates it, `dr_course.gd` builds it.

## A trap is a pure function of the tick and the tick it was pressed on

mg-wipeout's obstacles are functions of the tick alone, so nothing about them is sent. A trap has one more input, when its button was pressed, and **that is the only thing about a trap that travels**: one reliable `TRAP` event, the trap's index and the tick it went on. `DrCourse.extension(timing, seconds_since)` and `part_transform(part, e, running)` give every part's pose at every tick on both ends.

- **The event carries the PRESS's tick, not the arrival's.** A client that started the clock on arrival would draw and predict every trap a round trip late.
- A client that hears of a press after it has predicted past it re-poses the past on its next reconciliation, because the replay asks the course where the part was at each replayed tick, and the course now knows.
- A joiner's READY is answered with every trap already pressed and when.

**Killers are not solid; carriers are.** A lethal part is measured (`DrCourse.lethal_at`: five samples up the player's capsule against each part's box), never collided: a solid spike wall would push somebody out of itself before the test found them in it, in a direction that depends on resolution order, which two machines do not agree on. A solid moving part (a trapdoor leaf, a log, a falling block) carries whoever stands on it by the exact displacement `pose(t+dt)·pose(t)⁻¹·p − p`, inside the player's own tick, so a predicting client is carried exactly as the server carries it. **Whether a trap killed somebody is the server's**: a death arrives as an event, never predicted, because a client that decided it had died could decide it had not.

**Moving parts are STATIC bodies, teleported** (mg-wipeout's finding): a kinematic body's new transform lands a physics step late, and a replay of six ticks in one frame would sweep all six against a trapdoor a frame old.

**A trap is parts on a timeline, not a kind.** Every trap is `{button, timing {delay, out, hold, back}, parts [{at, size, move | turn | spin, solid, lethal, look}]}`. Trapdoors, crushers, closing walls, logs, falling blocks, sinking floes, jaws, icicles, saws, swinging blades and fire are all that, so the validator, builder, death test and carry each have one case. A lethal part that does not move is a hazard switched ON while the trap is out (fire) and drawn only then; one that moves is lethal wherever it is.

**Wind is a displacement, like the carry.** A part with `push` (`[x,y,z]` m/s, world axes) is a push zone: not solid, not lethal, drawn only while the trap is out (like fire). `DrCourse.push_at(feet, crouched, tick)` sums every running zone the capsule's middle is in, times one tick, and `DrPlayer._after_tick` adds it to the state beside the carry, so the server and a predicting client move the player identically. No dot-physics or dot-props volume was reused: those push simulated bodies, which two machines resolve in different orders.

## The tell

A trap waits `trap_tell_seconds` (0.35) after its button before it moves, and its parts flash red through it, so reading the activator is a skill; 0 restores the instant trap. **Written into the document, not into either end**: `DrGame.with_tell` adds it to every trap's `delay` (recording `timing.tell`) before the server builds and sends the course, so a client predicts the trap at exactly the server's tick. Only in `_lay_out_course`, where the catalogue's documents are pristine, so a rebuild never adds it twice. The flash is a `material_overlay` per part instance (`DrCourse._draw_tell`), drawn only: a trapdoor that shook would carry whoever stood on it.

## The course travels, whole, and is two corridors

The server reads a course, normalises it and sends it in `STAGE` (mg-wipeout's decision). **One document is the whole map**: the runners' corridor, the activators' walkway beside it with a button opposite every trap, the finish, and the end arena. A course with the arena in another file would be two documents that must agree where one stops.

**Courses are generated because the two corridors must agree along their whole length.** The builder lays a section (floor, trap, button, route points) at a cursor and moves on, then lays the walls and walkway the length of what was built. It refuses two buttons closer than 2.6 m (the nearer would be pressed for the further); `--check` fails if a file on disk is not what it would write.

- **The activator must be able to SEE the corridor.** The runners' wall on the walkway side stops level with the walkway; a thin rail and an invisible clip (`"visible": false`, a piece kind of its own) keep the activator in. `tools/shot.sh --view=activator` is the check.
- **Trap floors are drawn as floor.** A non-lethal trap part defaults to the ground's role and tint; a trapdoor in the moving-ground colour is one every runner walks round.
- **The walkway is straight**, because activator stand-ins walk straight to buttons.
- **Bunny-hop stretches** (the maps' `hops` section): platforms over a pit 2.4 m apart against a running jump of about 4.8 m (7 m/s, 1.2 m, gravity 20), clearable a landing at a time with auto-hop off (the default), built for chaining when `auto_bunny_hop` is on.

**Victim and spared spots.** Every trap names a `victim` spot that `headless_courses` springs it on; a trap that is dodged rather than jumped may also name `spared` (carried by `DrCourseDoc`, read by nothing in a round), where a runner must survive. The maps' arithmetic is in mg-deathrun-maps' own docs.

## How a trap sounds

`DrAudio.trap_sound` tells the machine from the parts, because a trap is parts and not a kind: a spinning part is a saw, fire a burner, a part that turns or a floor that moves away a trapdoor's bang, spikes closing sideways grind, spikes travelling up or down a heavy thud, anything else the impact. A mapper may write `"sound"` on the trap (`DrCourseDoc.TRAP_SOUNDS`). The five are synthesised into dot-audio's bank under the id and path a real file would have, so an `.ogg` in `audio/` still wins.

## Delivery of courses

The courses are their own repository and pack. `courses/` here is a dot-bootstrap link (`.gitignore` names it), and `game.yml` does not name the pack: a server owner names it in their deployment's map config (dot-server-deploy's `cfg/content.yml`, which fills `DotGameDescriptor.maps`). `DrModule._add_delivered_courses` asks `DotGameContent.map_dirs(server, "courses")` for every pack the server names and adds each mount's `courses/` to the catalogue at load **and again after every `dr_reload`**, because `load_from` forgets everything. The game's own pack carries only `dr_practice`, and courses are never in a client's content sync: the course reaches a client in `STAGE`. A new course reaches servers by a release of the maps repository and nothing else.
